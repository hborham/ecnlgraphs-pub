How to read a BeatGraph

A short tutorial for ECNL Graphs. No math required.

What you're looking at

Each graph shows one competition — one league, or one tournament. Every team is a badge. The best team sits at the top, and arrows point from a winner down to the teams it beat. Read an arrow as a sentence: A beat B. The graph is a picture of the season so far: who beat whom, all at once.

There is one graph per competition. Teams only appear with the opponents they actually played, so each graph stands on its own.

Three words to know

BeatWin. A direct win. When A beats B, A earns a BeatWin over B, weighted by the goal difference — winning 4–0 counts for more than winning 1–0. Draws create no BeatWin: nobody beat anybody.

BeatPath. A chain of BeatWins. If A beat B and B beat C, then A has a BeatPath to C, even if A and C never played each other. The whole ranking is built out of these chains.

BeatLoop. A win cycle: A beat B, B beat C, and C beat A. Nobody is clearly on top, so the loop has to be resolved before anyone can be ranked. The next section shows how.

How a loop gets resolved

Say the results are: A beat B by 7 goals, B beat C by 14, and C beat A by 3. That last result is the weakest link in the cycle, so its weight (3) is subtracted from every link in the loop:

LinkBeforeAfter
A beat B74
B beat C1411
C beat A3gone

The weakest result drops out, the other two survive with reduced weight, and the cycle is broken: A sits above B, B above C. The smallest loops are resolved first, and as little data as possible is removed. On the interactive views, the Broken Loops button shows you exactly which links were pruned.

Why some wins have no arrow

Only the smallest set of arrows is drawn. If A beat B and B beat C, the fact that A sits above C is already implied — drawing a third arrow would add clutter without adding information. So an arrow is left out whenever a BeatPath through other teams already says the same thing. The ratings still count every game; only the drawing is simplified. On one published example, 285 games reduced to 38 arrows.

On the interactive views, tapping a team highlights its BeatPaths and reveals its direct wins, including ones the drawing left out.

What the number means

Each team has a rating, roughly on a scale from −10 to 10. Higher means a stronger record against the teams in that competition: the rating counts every BeatPath in and out, weighted by goal difference, so beating strong teams matters more than beating weak ones.

Two cautions. The rating describes what has already happened — it does not predict the next game. And ratings only compare teams inside the same competition: a 6.5 in one league and a 6.5 in another were earned against different opponents, so they are not directly comparable.

Gold rings, empty graphs, and scores

Gold rings mark bracket teams, and only appear on tournament graphs (Surf Cup). In a league every team is in the competition, so rings would be meaningless and are left off.

Empty graphs mean the schedule is posted but no games have been scored yet. Those graphs fill in on their own as results come in.

Scores sit under every graph on the home view, and on the interactive views each team's results appear when you tap it.

The three views

Graphs — the home view: the graph as a picture, with the full scores table as readable text. Swimlane — the interactive D3 view: teams in rank layers, tap a team for its details. Canvas — the interactive Cytoscape view: the same graph on a canvas you can pan and zoom. All three show the same data and the same filters, and switching between them keeps your selection.

Use the five filters — Season, Gender, Age, Type, Competition — to pick a competition. The page address holds your selection, so you can copy the link to share exactly what you're looking at.

Where it comes from

The game data comes from the league schedules (Total Global Sports) and the Surf Cup results site, and the graphs are rebuilt weekly. The ranking method is the Weighted Method from BeatGraph. The interactive views are built with D3, dagre, and Cytoscape.js.
